6. IV Crush Demonstration

Earnings events can cause implied volatility to fall sharply after the uncertainty surrounding the event is resolved.
Key concept A long straddle can lose value after an earnings announcement if implied volatility falls substantially and the realised stock-price movement is not large enough to compensate for the volatility repricing and time decay.
Before expiration:

Option value ≠ expiration payoff

Option value reflects factors such as:
• stock price
• strike
• time to expiration
• implied volatility
• interest rate

At expiration:

Value = intrinsic payoff

7. Volatility & Option Greeks

Implied volatility (IV)

The volatility level embedded in an option's market price. It represents the market's forward-looking volatility expectation under the pricing framework.

Realised volatility

The volatility actually observed in the underlying stock over a period. A high IV does not guarantee that realised volatility will be high enough to make a straddle profitable.

Vega

Measures the sensitivity of an option's theoretical value to a change in implied volatility. A long straddle is generally long vega, so higher IV tends to increase the theoretical value of both long options, all else equal.

Theta

Measures sensitivity to the passage of time. Long options generally have negative theta, meaning time decay tends to reduce their value when other factors are unchanged.

Gamma

Measures the rate at which delta changes as the stock price changes. A long straddle is generally long gamma, meaning large stock-price movements can materially change the position's delta exposure.

Expiration payoff

At expiration there is no remaining time value. The call and put values become their intrinsic payoffs, after which the premiums paid are subtracted to determine strategy P/L.

8. Strategy Formula Audit

Call payoff = max(Sₜ − Kcall, 0)

Put payoff = max(Kput − Sₜ, 0)

Long Straddle P/L = Call payoff + Put payoff − Call premium − Put premium

Total premium = Call premium + Put premium

Lower breakeven = Strike − Total premium

Upper breakeven = Strike + Total premium

Maximum loss = Total premium

Maximum loss per standard contract = Total premium × 100

Maximum profit: Upside profit is unlimited because the call has unlimited upside potential. Downside profit is limited because NVIDIA's stock price cannot fall below zero.
